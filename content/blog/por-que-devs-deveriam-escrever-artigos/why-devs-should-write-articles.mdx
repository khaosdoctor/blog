I was going through the topics on [DEV Community](https://dev.to/khaosdoctor) recently and found a [great article](https://dev.to/iriskatastic/writing-technical-articles-tips-for-programmers-29gp) with tips for people who want to start writing technical articles. So I decided to write my own take on the subject and reference some parts of that excellent content too.

> _Some of the tips and references below are copied from that article purely for practical reasons, so the reader doesn't have to go hunt for the original content. The opinions on them, though, are my own._

## Why write?

Everyone, programmer or not, should write about what they do and what they're learning, or just write about the life they're living.

There's an interesting point in that article that we don't think about much. Whenever we read technical content on the Internet, we associate the creator of that content with authority, or at least some authority, on the subject, and that's extremely positive for your career and for you as a person.

> _Being associated with experts on a subject is always a good practice. When people read your article on the Internet, they recognize you as a professional and trust what you say more.__\- Taken from the [_reference_](https://dev.to/iriskatastic/writing-technical-articles-tips-for-programmers-29gp)_

And this is, among others, one of the reasons why you, as a developer, should start writing technical content. Consolidating your status as a development professional can help your future career and also influences how you handle situations. Someone who writes regularly picks up skills that matter for the job market:

-   **Improves your spoken and written communication**: which improves how you deal with clients and colleagues, and also helps you build and present arguments more clearly and concisely.
-   **Gives you a new way to think**: The process of writing an article is quite elaborate, as we'll get into further down, it demands organization and a concrete way of building things. You're not going to manage to write anything if you just throw your ideas into the air. Thinking about how to write is a great way to start seeing the world differently.
-   **Prepares you for feedback**: When we put ourselves out there on the Internet we're exposed to every kind of response, good or bad, and it's important to know how to handle both. This develops the skill of responding to criticism thoughtfully and taking compliments with humility.

We've already covered a lot of what writing develops as a skill, but beyond that, why should we start writing?

### It consolidates your learning

You've probably heard the phrase: _"You only really learn something when you teach it to someone else,"_ or some variation of it, right?

That's real. Teaching someone else forces you to look for connections and consolidate your own knowledge. A pretty simple way to do that is by writing about it.

Writing forces you to think about the simplest and easiest way to make the person reading understand the content you want to get across, and that helps consolidate your experience and knowledge quickly and easily.

Or, if you're like me, your motivation for writing articles can come simply from wanting to share knowledge with other people and wanting to observe and analyze points of view and opinions different from your own.

### It puts you in front of the world

As a professional, it's important that other people know what you're doing and what you stand out at. What do you want people to know you for? If you dream of working at a certain company but don't know how to get there, one of the options is exactly that: **show up more**. After all, every company is made up of people, and those people need to know you exist.

Beyond the professional advantage, writing also puts you, inevitably, on a level where, in the eyes of others, you become a reference on whatever you're talking about, since a lot of people will look for your content to complete school assignments or even build their own personal projects.

To this day I remember that, back in 2010, when I was starting to learn to program and, more often than not, would land on the great [José Carlos Macoratti](http://macoratti.net/Default.aspx)'s site, back when I was coding in [.NET](https://docs.microsoft.com/dotnet/core/?WT.mc_id=blog-devto-ludossan), he's still one of my biggest references, even though I haven't touched a line of [C#](https://docs.microsoft.com/dotnet/csharp/?WT.mc_id=blog-devto-ludossan) in almost ten years. Remember Isaac Newton's iconic line:

> _If I have seen further it is by standing on the shoulders of giants__\- Isaac Newton_

You can be one of the giants that other giants have relied on to develop their creations. You'll probably never find out, but other people will, maybe that HR manager at the company you're dying to work for.

### It captures your thoughts

Last but not least, writing creates a knowledge repository that can be the difference between a project's success or failure. You don't need to write with the goal of becoming famous, but rather to record your knowledge for yourself, building an archive of useful information you can search whenever you need it.

> _A personal example, one that's already saved me more than once, is my [_notes repository_](https://github.com/khaosdoctor/my-notes), a simple place where, for more than three years, I've written down pretty much everything I've learned from every course I've ever taken. In there you'll find sample repositories, tips and tricks, ideas, and even [_entire talk outlines_](https://github.com/khaosdoctor/my-notes/blob/master/node/Guideline%20de%20palestra%20the%20conf%202019.pdf)._

Writing about something publicly, or even privately, makes you record and hold a written mental documentation of that moment. Think of it as a _snapshot_ of your brain at a given point in time.

## Why don't devs write?

We're devs, not journalists or reporters, and definitely not professional writers. Everything conspires to keep a dev from ever writing anything. Among the biggest problems devs face when it comes to writing:

-   **Shyness**: Devs are naturally introverted people, often that's exactly why we picked this profession, machines are a lot simpler to deal with than people, and that hits hard when it comes to expressing yourself to the whole world through the Internet. But it can be overcome. A really cool example is the article by [Mariane Santana](https://dev.to/marianesantana/promises-in-15-minutes-9l7), who had never written before and is now [on the list of the 7 most read articles](https://dev.to/devteam/the-7-most-popular-dev-posts-from-the-past-week-a8o) on DEV Community 🤘
-   **Planning**: Devs generally run high on anxiety, but there's no point trying to write everything at once, we need a plan
-   **Lack of practice**: Unfortunately there's not much we can change here except say _practice makes perfect…_ You'll only get better at writing by practicing writing more and more. Reading also helps a lot with growing your writing vocabulary and improving how you write
-   **Fear of being criticized**: As I mentioned, when we expose ourselves on the Internet, we're subject to every kind of criticism, so a lot of people don't write out of fear of being harshly criticized. Not much to do here except be fully confident in what you're writing and feel ready to answer questions, not engaging is also an option.

## How to write an article

### Pick a topic

You can literally write about **anything**. How about your experience using a tool? Or your learning process? Maybe about [how studying music influenced your life as a dev](https://dev.to/womakerscode/career-in-programming-and-music-what-have-i-learned-from-all-this-3a7h). Anyway, it's your call!

Nobody expects you to write about something outside your area, let alone be the ultimate authority on something you're still learning (as long as you make it clear you're learning). That might make you uncomfortable, but on the flip side, having a perspective on how **YOU** do things and what **YOUR** routine looks like can help other people figure out how to be better. Points of view… always…

Other writing ideas:

-   Your own experiences on a topic
-   News about your favorite technologies
-   Life stories, yours or someone else's
-   Lessons, failures, successes and takeaways from your journey

### Research and plan

A really important step in technical writing is research. You're probably not going to be the first person to write about something. But research your topic _really well_ before you start writing. Also have a plan for what you're going to write:

-   **Schedule your articles**: Like I said [in my series on personal organization](https://dev.to/khaosdoctor/o-guia-definitivo-da-organizacao-pessoal-parte-1-44ea), everything needs to become a planned task, and articles are no different, plan your dates in advance
-   **Know your topic**: Know in advance _what_ you want to write about
-   **Plan your content**: Write, anywhere, an _outline_ of your article, with the main headings and topics

**Check whether there are other articles on the same topic.** There's nothing wrong with writing about the same thing someone else has, exactly like I'm doing here, just make sure it's not plagiarism and always link back to the original content. Here are a few angles:

-   Your personal experience with the topic
-   A different opinion
-   An addendum to the topic (like this article)
-   A translation into another language

Look for as many distinct points of view on the same subject as you can. The more opinions you bring in, the richer your article gets.

### Picking a title

Your article's title is what gets people interested in reading it. So it needs to be **well thought out**. The title should be clear, concise, and should signal the article's purpose. I'll use some translated examples from the [reference article](https://dev.to/iriskatastic/writing-technical-articles-tips-for-programmers-29gp#naming):

1.  _Roundup #58: Orleans 3.0, Snitch, What's Your Problem? The Next 5 Years of ASP.NET Core_
2.  _The Programming Language to Rule Them All_
3.  _Suffering From Technical Debt?_

Here's why these examples are bad:

1.  Too many keywords, too much going on, the title is too long and seems to be about absolutely everything
2.  Too "esoteric", it clearly reads as _clickbait_, but doesn't give much of a hint about what the content actually covers (this also applies to #3)
3.  A question aimed at the reader doesn't make a good title. It raises doubt, and that creates the expectation of an answer. If the answer you give doesn't satisfy the reader, you've just tarnished your reputation with that specific person.

Now some good examples:

1.  [Career in Programming and Music: What Have I Learned From All This?](https://dev.to/womakerscode/career-in-programming-and-music-what-have-i-learned-from-all-this-3a7h)
2.  [Building a Node.js CRUD Application with PostgreSQL](https://dev.to/azure/desenvolvendo-uma-aplicacao-crud-node-js-com-postgresql-3clk)
3.  [Tutorial: Data Pipeline Using MongoDB and Kafka Connect on Kubernetes](https://dev.to/azure/tutorial-data-pipeline-using-mongodb-and-kafka-connect-on-kubernetes-eio)

> _Notice that [_Ana_](https://dev.to/anabneri)'s article on music has a question in it, but it's not aimed at the reader, it's a rhetorical question pointed at herself, explaining what the article is about._

### Structure

Every article follows a line of reasoning, something we learn in school in our first essays. Every piece of text has:

-   Title
-   Introduction
-   Body (this is where code, images and examples go)
-   Conclusion
-   References

Technical articles are no different, the only thing that changes a bit is the _References_ part, which is usually used as a section where you can tie an article to the rest of your work.

### Length

A [Data Lab article](https://medium.com/data-lab/the-optimal-post-is-7-minutes-74b9f41509b) showed that the ideal reading time for an article is seven to ten minutes. That's not a rule, but it helps guide how long your content should be. Personally, I use [an extension](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=gabrielepmattia.wordcount&WT.mc_id=blog-devto-ludossan) for [VSCode](https://code.visualstudio.com/?WT.mc_id=blog-devto-ludossan) that shows me this information.

That said, there's nothing wrong with going over that time if you're writing a more detailed article or a tutorial, after all, it's your content. Just consider whether it's worth splitting it into more than one, forming a series, like I do [with a lot of my articles](https://dev.to/khaosdoctor)

### Content

People expect to read the content you said you'd deliver. Avoid overly complex words, or writing like it's a scientific paper, unless, of course, that's the point.

Include images whenever you can, [gists](https://gist.github.com/), and use [Carbon](https://carbon.now.sh/) or [CodePen](https://codepen.io/) to embed code.

A few more content tips:

-   Avoid being completely informal, but also avoid being too formal
-   Look at apps like Grammarly (if you're writing in English), lean on text-analysis extensions
-   Always proofread your text before publishing
-   See if someone else can read the text so it gets a second pair of eyes
-   Expand acronyms the first time they show up in the article, for example: _"The use of [_AKS_](https://docs.microsoft.com/azure/aks/?WT.mc_id=blog-devto-ludossan) (Azure Kubernetes Service) …"_
-   Prefer using first person plural when writing articles, unless it's an opinion piece, because "we" builds a sense of togetherness with the reader
-   Don't write overly long paragraphs
-   Whenever you provide data (numbers, etc), cite the date and source they came from

### Keywords

Keywords matter because that's how search engines index your text. To look for which tags you can use, here are some direct examples from the article I referenced:

-   moz.com
-   app.neilpatel.com
-   trends.google.com
-   keywordtool.io
-   wordtracker.com/scout

Use at least 5 tags to indicate what you're writing about, going from more generic to more specific, for example: `open-source, back-end, javascript, node.js, cli`

### Promotion

Don't be afraid to promote your work as much as you can, use tools like [MLabs](https://mlabs.com.br/), [Hootsuite](https://hootsuite.com/) and others to manage your most-used social networks, schedule your posts. Use social media groups that specialize in what you're writing about, your audience is there.

> **_Tip__: Keep posting about your article regularly, but avoid duplicating the text, a lot of social network algorithms flag duplicate posts on the same account as spam._**
