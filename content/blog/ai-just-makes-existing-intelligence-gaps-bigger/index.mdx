> [!note] Small core argument (AI generated so I don't forget)
> AI is a **multiplier, not a equalizer**. It amplifies what's already there — if you have good judgment, critical thinking, and domain knowledge, AI makes you dramatically more capable. But if you lack those things, AI just confidently accelerates your bad decisions and blind spots.
>
> The dangerous part isn't the dumb people knowing they're dumb — it's the **Dunning-Kruger layer**: AI gives everyone the _feeling_ of competence, so people who don't know what they don't know now have a very polished, fast, confident tool helping them be wrong at scale.
>
> The productivity narrative is a **lie by omission** — yeah, output goes up, but quality of thinking can go down if the person wasn't already equipped to evaluate what AI gives them.
>
> So the real message is: **AI has no floor**. The ceiling rises for smart people, but there's no safety net for the rest — and nobody's talking about that.
>
> The article's real punch is probably this: **AI removed the natural friction that used to force people to improve.** Bad writing got rejected. Bad code didn't compile. Bad decisions had consequences. Now there's a layer of polish and speed on top of everything that masks the rot underneath.
