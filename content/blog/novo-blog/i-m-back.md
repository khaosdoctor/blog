I don't know if you noticed, but my last post on this blog was in March 2025! And it was a Backlog newsletter![^1] And now, more than a year later, I decided it's time to come back to the blog, and finally have the decency to rebuild it with a more interesting theme that isn't just a _copy paste_ of some generic theme from a stock CMS[^2].

I decided to write this text today because, first, I needed to test how the blog was working, since I changed basically my whole article production pipeline. Second, because I think I owe an explanation to whoever read/used to read/reads this blog, and one to myself too.

Finally, it was really nice to build this site again in a more open way and less tied to a CMS like the Ghost I was using before. So it'll be interesting to show you some of the things I built here in other posts in this series.

But first...

## Why did I disappear?

I could list a bunch of excuses and say it was "*because life happened*", or that *"I got too busy"*. But the reality is that I lost the joy of writing for a good while. Actually I lost the will to share content in general for a good while. One of the reasons for that was the pressure of always having to release something new.

Because of programs like Microsoft MVP, Google Developer Expert, Docker Captains, etc. We, as participants, need to report what our activities are throughout the year, which is fair, since we were awarded to produce content and we're not producing that content, but for me it became a bit much. I've been part of these programs since 2017[^9], that's almost 10 years of constant content production with those reports. And I've been producing content since 2013, which is a lot more content before that.

I'm not saying these programs *force* you to produce anything, quite the opposite, nobody says anything and nobody makes you do anything, the problem is that I force myself to do these things like it's a religion and that ended up taking away more than the joy, but also _the will_ to create anything else.

It got to a point where I was looking for content to post to the point of writing "for the sake of writing", not because I *actually* wanted to write that content. And that's when I realized I couldn't keep going and decided to stop until I sorted myself out. Which took much longer than expected.

During all that time I stopped completely with all content, not just text, I stopped giving talks, making YouTube videos, literally everything. And it was a very important period for me, because I found out what I really *like*, and it also gave me a new view of what I really *want* to do. But, more important than that:

==I learned to be okay with myself producing only what I like==

For a long time I thought the amount of things I did was more important than the quality of the things I do. And I punished myself a lot for not producing enough content, I constantly compared myself with other creators who were producing more, having more audience, it was always a race, against me and against everyone else as if we were all in some kind of constant competition.[^4]

All of that added up gave me a very strong anxiety attack, and that was the moment I realized I wasn't well enough to keep going. And that's why I decided to take a break. And that break was excellent for some introspection, I got to pay more attention to myself, to my health, to my family and friends, spend more time with my cats and so on.

But let's expand a bit on what went down.

## What happened during this time

During all the time I was away, I had plenty of room and moments to think about what I actually like doing and also about what I was already doing. Besides making peace a bit more with the whole situation of the dreaded __artificial intelligence__[^5]. 

So let's go in parts.

### What we think of ourselves

I'm not going to expand on AI here, but I need to say a few things to give some context. 

When AI became more present in devs' lives and it was, in fact, being a good tool that created working and even useful things, I caught myself thinking:

> [!quote] Me, when I didn't want to write anymore
> Why write about technology when you can now create anything so fast that people [don't even bother understanding it anymore](https://unix.foo/posts/last-people-who-know-how-it-works/)?

I won't say I wasn't super upset about that, after all I always wrote about technology and I loved teaching people to do new things with it, I also used this same blog a lot as a way to remember everything I learned over the years (I've always been a fan of [learning in public](https://github.lsantos.dev/my-notes)), but it made no sense at all (at least to me) to keep going, since I or anyone else could just write a prompt in Claude and have everything ready, nobody would need to understand anything anymore.

And that's when something occurred to me: **I** defined myself as a technology guy, but I'm not _only_ a technology guy, I can talk about infinite things.

I like to define myself as someone with serial curiosity[^8-nota-de-tradução]. Which is a pretty way of saying that I'm interested in _anything_, literally, from cooking[^7] to astronomy. Most of the time that's really nice, because I become someone very interesting in bar conversations, but at the same time, having _so many_ interests ends up charging a high price. Especially from someone who already judges themselves as not being enough.

That's why I turned my hobbies into work, I always said I _"had to create a post"_, or _"had to create some content"_. But, the reality is that:

==We're not obligated to do absolutely anything. That's imposed on us by ourselves out of other people's expectations.==

And that was the main thing I realized. That I, Lucas, don't need to prove to myself or to anyone that something I'm doing is worth it or not, actually nobody does. We should only do what makes us happy regardless of what anyone else says.

Even if those things lead to absolutely nothing.

### New passions

I made the decision for this blog that, from now on, it's going to be more than a technology blog, it's going to be my personal place to voice my opinions about everything I've been thinking about. Whether about technology or not.

I had already done this for Backlog in the past, where I talked about history, opinions, technology, and several other things. But this time I'm going to turn this blog into that space, like it should have been from the start.

To be honest, I still don't feel like talking about software development proper again. But this time away made me realize I have other passions I'd love to share with people, for example, I always wanted to learn electronics but never had the willpower to start learning. But during this period I not only started learning but also started building things[^10], I started getting more involved with music[^11], I started understanding that technology isn't just what I used to work with, it isn't just the Web, there's much more beyond that.

And that's why I think this blog, now on top of everything, is much more worth it. So expect diverse content about several other things here, I don't have a posting schedule, and I won't have one, the posts will come out when they come out. Starting with a post about what the new blog can do!

But I don't want to go on too long about this now, we'll have specific posts for each of these things!

See you around!
