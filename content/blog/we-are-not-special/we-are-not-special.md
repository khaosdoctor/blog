This post originally came from a paragraph of the [[my-relationship-with-ai-at-the-moment|other article in this series]] because I thought it was too big of a topic to be left aside. But, why do programmers think so highly about themselves?

Really, have you ever stopped to observe that the general perception of that hivemind we call "software engineers" is usually out of the charts? Obviously, not all the cases are extreme, but I usually don't see that in other professions.

Lately, with AI and all the advancements, I see that some people are losing their minds over the fact that AI just showed what I stated before, and instead of adapting and evolving with it, solving the problem like the problem solvers we are, some still prefer to deny all of it and just keep the dream alive.

I heard this quote somewhere:

> [!quote] Someone I heard once and loved the quote
> Programming is the closest thing we have to a superpower

And that's true, but it doesn't make programmers superheroes. We have always thought about ourselves as some sort of "master race" because we could understand *the sacred scriptures of the machines*. Think about it. Compared to a "normal job", programmers have:

- More job stability[^1]
- Work often less hours
- Way bigger salaries
- Super comfortable work conditions

And we *still* complain a lot. I know people who'd only dream about working from home all days while earning a salary up to 6 (or even 7!) digits per year for editing fancy text files. And we have it because we know what files to edit and what to put in those fancy text files. We were the keepers of a black box called "code" that everyone but us didn't "dare to touch".

But... Being completely honest (and realistic), we are absolutely not special. **Code has never been the issue, understanding has**. We keep bitching about how "no one understands the code anymore", "no one know anything", but, if we actually think about it, how much do *we* know about the things we are using? How much do you know about compilers? How much do you know about this very browser?

So, the code evolved, it's easier, faster, almost [[your-code-is-worthless|worthless]]. But **understanding it**, is exactly the same problem it was 60 years ago. Only the means have changed. The goal is the same: **to solve the problem**.

This is why I stand on the point that every project, as small as it is, needs to have it's knowledge written down and passed on. Now, with AI, more than ever we are in need of documentation and knowledge sharing.

AI can be an enemy, or it can be a tool. Use it to explain the code it just made back to you and write about how it works. But **read it!** Understand it! Be **reasonable** to tell the difference between what needs to be understood fully and what you can cut some corners, we are engineers, that's what we do.

Remember, not all code needs to be perfect, but most code needs to be documented so somewhere, sometime, someone will be able to make sense of what you built.

## The AI and us

To end this, I wanted to just say something in the lines of: *"Stop bitching around and be a real engineer"* but I think that would sound too harsh. So, to get my point across I will make an analogy.

You don't see painters refusing to work on a project because it was painted using a new type of brush. But somehow programmers (me included) would defend their tools more than their own family. And this is really not what programming is about, AI is just another tool, AI is just another thing we are using to achieve the end that made us come to this field in the first place: **to make things work**.

It took me a long time to drop the bone of "my stack vs your stack". We are a builders, we are makers, we are problem solvers, no matter how the problem comes about.

If you care that much about the *how* or the tool, then it's sad to say that you might have to adjust harder to this change, otherwise it will run over you as it has ran over many others in the past.
