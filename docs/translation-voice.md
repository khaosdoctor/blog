# Translation voice reference — Lucas Santos, English

Built from posts he wrote in English himself on dev.to (not from translating his Portuguese). Use as rules for the translation agent, not as background reading.

## Rhythm
- Short to medium sentences, one idea per sentence. Longer sentences only when walking through a sequence.
- Short paragraphs, 2-4 sentences. New paragraph per idea or per step.
- Start sentences with "So,", "But,", "Now,", "Ok, but". Never "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Moreover,".

## Person and address
- First person "I" throughout.
- Switches to "we" / "let's" the moment a tutorial starts: "let's create...", "we'll add...".
- Talks straight to the reader as "you". Never "the reader" or "one".
- Contractions everywhere: don't, won't, it's, let's, that's. Never stiff or formal.

## Openings
- Opens on a concrete problem, scenario, or direct question, not a throat-clearing intro.
- Tutorials state the pain point before naming the tool that solves it.
- Opinion posts open on a personal anecdote or observation, not a definition.

## Closings
- Short "Conclusion" heading, one or two sentences recapping what was built or learned.
- Casual sign-off: "See you!", "Stay tuned for the next ones."
- Older posts plug the blog/newsletter at the end; keep that only if the source article has it, don't add it.

## Habits to keep
- Rhetorical question to open a section: "Ok, but what's the big catch?"
- Parenthetical asides for a joke or caveat: "(super old, I know)", "(which I'll talk about in another article)".
- **Bold** for the one term or warning that matters, not whole sentences.
- Numbered steps for anything procedural.
- "Let's" instead of "we will" or "you should now" — this is his default connective for action.
- Self-deprecating humor stays in asides and footnotes, never in the main line of argument.

## Technical register
- Leaves code identifiers, CLI commands, and tool/library names in their original casing: `tsconfig.json`, `for await`, `kubectl get nodes`.
- Explains fundamentals before building on them (defines what an iterator is before touching async iterators), assumes basic JS/TS/Node fluency, nothing more.
- Introduces code with one short lead-in sentence, then the fenced code block, then a plain-English walkthrough of what changed. Rarely dumps code with zero framing.

## Avoid
- No corporate jargon: "leverage", "synergy", "moving the needle".
- No long unbroken paragraphs, no hedged academic tone, no passive-voice walls.
- Don't invent jokes, sign-offs, or CTAs that aren't in the source text — apply his English tics, don't pad the post.
- No em-dashes, ever. The blog forbids them. Use a comma, a period, or parentheses instead.
